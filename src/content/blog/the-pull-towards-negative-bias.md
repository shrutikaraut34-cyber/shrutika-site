A major challenge that people face nowadays is rising above the overwhelming negativity we see everywhere. Whether it be in real life or online, there seems to be a rising trend towards negative thinking. The volatility of public reaction almost always leads to the use of salacious information to draw people in. Who did something wrong, who had the wrong take, who cheated on whom, who dissed whom - the prompts are endless. We have become more direct and careless with our opinions, and negativity has given rise to a black-and-white view of society.

Controversial or sensational topics of conversation naturally increase engagement, which is why there are more biased and exaggerated news headlines, reports, videos, and information around us. This is a documented change in the digital landscape. Clickbait, misleading premises, and even straight-up lies are often found on the internet. Even our digital devices and algorithms exploit the natural human tendency to retain negative details more than the positive. Scandal tends to generate more discourse than positive or neutral information. And the more we click, share, and comment on these stories, the more our feeds learn to serve us the same thing, quietly pulling us deeper into the cycle.

## What is negativity bias?

This phenomenon is known as negativity bias, where our brain is more likely to remember pessimistic information, events, thoughts, and even memories. This is an evolutionary tendency, and historically it helped us be more wary of dangers. But now, the informational overload of the digital world has affected how we think. Many people have observed a tendency in themselves to think the worst straight out of the gate. A tool that was once meant to protect us has slowly turned into a constant, low-level source of stress.

A lot of people think that just consuming negative content doesn't contribute to negativity bias in them. Research into doomscrolling shows otherwise, such as how digital content consumption can cause a form of vicarious trauma and diminish our faith in humanity. Just because something bad didn't happen to us doesn't mean it doesn't affect us. Our empathy can cause us to have a visceral reaction to negative events we see online - which can be caused by our own similar experiences or vivid imaginations. Over time, this steady stream of secondhand distress can leave us feeling anxious, drained or hopeless, even when nothing is wrong in our own lives.

Another element of negativity bias is rumination. Once the brain falls into a negativity cycle, it is easy to become laser-focused on what confirms our bias. And rumination only worsens the effects - the more negative fodder our brain has, the more it circles around them. It is increasingly difficult to escape this because of the informational onslaught that we receive from dawn to dusk - especially if we spend too much time on social media. Before we know it, a single negative thought can snowball into an entire mood, coloring the way we look at everything around us.

## Do you have a negativity bias?

To test if you have a negativity bias, you can evaluate yourself through the following steps:

1. Look back on your day and notice whether you remember more good or bad moments.
2. Think about one bad experience and one good experience from your life. If the bad one comes to mind faster, or feels stronger than the good one, this bias may affect you too.
3. Look at what your apps show you. If your feed keeps recommending angry or controversial content, that can strongly shape your thinking too.

None of these are meant to make you feel bad - they are simply a way to notice a pattern you might not have realized was there.

## How to escape the negativity

So how can you escape all the negativity around you? The first step is always to realize it yourself and find the source of the problem. It could be the people around you and the media you take in. But it can also come from within, like past memories and events that left a lasting mark on you. Looking for these causes and connections in your own mind can become your own little adventure. Understanding why you think the way you do is often the first real step to changing it.

The truth is, changing your negative thoughts is mostly up to you - which is both a hopeful and a scary thought. One of the main ways is to change your digital habits. No matter how detached you think you are, your mind still takes in a lot of information you don't notice, and that can shape your thinking without you even knowing. So changing what you take in is a must. One simple way is to stop engaging with content that makes you react too strongly. Click "not interested," or just swipe away. Instead, try to focus on content that makes you think, question, and grow in a good way.

The next way is to "take in the good." Give yourself 10 to 20 seconds to notice and enjoy anything that makes you feel good. We often forget to appreciate the small things that make us happy. Some people even feel that focusing on something good, like a compliment or a small win, makes them selfish or full of themselves. This also manifests in the inability to take in positive information or process positive emotions. It's important to notice and sit with the good feelings we have every day. This helps our brain focus on the good things in life. Over time, we can train ourselves to be more positive people.

If you want a step-by-step approach, you can also try the "low bad diet." According to the book *The Power of Bad*, negativity has a very strong hold on people's minds and choices. Being on a "low bad diet" simply means limiting how much bad information we let in. You can follow these steps:

1. Actively limit how much negative news and social media you take in.
2. For every negative story or moment, look for four good or positive ones to balance it out.
3. Listen to people who focus on solutions and good news instead of spreading fear.

There are many more ways to reduce your negativity bias, such as counseling, journaling, practising gratitude, saying affirmations out loud, and more. All of these are meant to make you more thoughtful and self-aware. The more space you make for positivity in your mind, the more it spreads into every part of your life.
